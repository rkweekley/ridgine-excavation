Excavation & Land Clearing in St. Clairsville, Ohio

St. Clairsville is the Belmont County seat on I-70, where shale and coal-measure rock sit just under the surface and every slope demands drainage. Ridgeline Excavation cuts pads, opens lanes and manages water on this ground — from hillside homesites to commercial pads along the corridor.

Rocky shale ground and what it means for your site

Belmont County sits on shale and coal-measure rock that's close to the surface, so digging here means hitting rock sooner than in river-bottom counties. That's not a problem — shale breaks up with the right machine — but the pad, the lane and the drainage all need to account for rock from the start. We grade and compact on top of it, so your base won't settle unevenly.

Hillside building sites and switchback lanes

St. Clairsville's best buildable lots are often on slopes — the flat ground near the interstate is largely spoken for. Hillside sites need a cut pad and a drive that climbs without washing out. We cut into the slope, build a lane with cross-drainage at every switchback, and grade so runoff never pools against the foundation. For commercial pads along the corridor, we handle the larger cuts and the stormwater the county requires.

Drainage that holds up on sloped lots

Water moves fast on a slope, and if it isn't managed, it takes soil and gravel with it. We install French drains behind retaining walls, cut swales above the pad, and set culverts where the drive crosses the drainage path. Every sloped site gets a drainage plan specific to its grade.

Excavation questions St. Clairsville landowners ask us

How does all the shale in our ground change the excavation?

Shale means the dig is rockier and takes more machine time than plain soil, but once it's broken up and compacted it makes a solid base. We'll tell you what we're hitting as we open the ground, and we grade on top of it so your pad stays firm.

Can you cut a building pad into a hillside and keep it from sliding?

We cut into the slope, bench the pad, and install drainage behind and around it — French drains, swales above the cut, and positive grade away on all sides. If the slope needs a retaining wall, we'll tell you up front.

We need a switchback drive up to our build site — can you design and cut it?

Switchback lanes on shale slopes need cross-drainage at every turn and a crowned surface that sheds water the whole way down. We cut the alignment, grade the turns wide enough for material trucks, and set culverts where the lane crosses drainage paths.

Can you prep a commercial pad near the I-70 corridor?

Yes. We handle larger cuts for commercial and light-industrial pads along the corridor, including the stormwater management and grading the county reviews. We'll walk the site, confirm what the ground needs, and give you a written estimate.
