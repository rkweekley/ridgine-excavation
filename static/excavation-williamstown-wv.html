Where two rivers set the terms

The ground in Williamstown changes fast with elevation. Down by the water it's flat, soft ground that floods; a few blocks uphill you're on steep, wooded lots where soil is thin over rock and a small cut changes how the slope drains. Both build, but each wants a different approach from the first shovel.

We start every job by walking the site and understanding where water is coming from and where it needs to go. On river-bottom ground that means raising the pad and shaping the grade so runoff has a clear path out. On a hillside it means staging the cut, then getting drainage in before the ground erodes.

Clearing hillsides and building on river ground

A lot of Williamstown projects start as a wooded lot tucked into one of the ridges above town. Clearing takes more than cutting trees — the stumps and roots hold the slope together, and pulling them changes how water moves across it. We clear in stages, get drainage in early, and shape the finish so rain doesn't wash topsoil down to the street.

Down closer to the river the jobs are different: regrading a lot that's held water, cutting a driveway into a soft parcel, or putting in a compacted pad for a garage or workshop. We work the same way in Marietta across the bridge, Vienna to the south, and the acreage toward Lowell.

Questions we hear around Williamstown

Can you build on ground that floods near the river?

It's buildable, but it usually means raising the pad above the flood level and grading so water has a clear exit path. We'll look at the site and tell you what's needed before any dirt moves.

How do you clear a steep wooded lot without causing erosion?

We clear in stages — trees and brush first, stumps left until we're ready to grade that section, and drainage in as soon as the ground is opened.

Can your equipment get down these old narrow streets?

Williamstown has tight streets in the older sections near the river. We size the machine to the access and plan the route in advance to protect curbs, trees, and neighboring property.

What kind of soil are we dealing with down by the river?

Mostly silt and clay — it holds water and compresses if it isn't handled right. We compact in lifts, add stone where the subgrade is soft, and grade so water moves away instead of soaking in.
