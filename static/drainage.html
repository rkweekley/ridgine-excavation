Drainage Work That Actually Fixes the Water Problem

Standing water doesn't go away on its own — on Mid-Ohio Valley clay it just hangs around and slowly works against your foundation, driveway and lawn. We design and build drainage that moves the water off your property for good.

Why Water Sits On Your Land

Most of the Mid-Ohio Valley sits on clay-heavy soil that holds water instead of letting it soak through. Add a high water table along the Ohio River bottom, heavy spring runoff coming off a hillside, and the freeze-thaw cycles that clog older drains, and the water has nowhere to go. It pools in the yard, seeps toward your foundation, and cuts gullies through your driveway.

A French drain, a properly graded swale, or a new culvert gives that water a clear route away from the things you're trying to protect. Done right, it works quietly for years. Done halfway, it simply moves the problem to a new spot.

How a Drainage Job Gets Planned and Built

Every drainage job starts with two questions: where the water is coming from, and where it's legally allowed to go. We walk the property after a rain to see how the water actually moves, then choose the fix that fits — a swale to carry surface water, a French drain to pull groundwater out of the soil, or a culvert to pass water under a driveway or lane.

  • French drains and curtain drains to dry out a wet yard or a basement wall
  • Swales and drainage ditches graded to carry spring runoff away from the house
  • Culvert installation under driveways, lanes, and low crossings
  • Regrading low spots so water can't pool in the first place

Signs You Need to Fix the Water

  • Standing water in the yard for days after a rain
  • A damp basement or crawlspace after heavy weather
  • Washouts and ruts forming in the driveway each spring
  • A lawn or field that stays spongy and won't dry out

Common Drainage Questions

Will a French drain solve standing water in my yard?

Usually, yes — if it's placed correctly. A French drain collects groundwater in a buried pipe and carries it to an outlet. The key is getting the slope and the drain location right.

What causes water in my basement?

Most basement water is surface water that isn't being carried away. When downspouts empty against the foundation or the ground slopes toward the house, water collects at the wall and finds a way in. Fixing the grade and rerouting that water is usually the first and cheapest step.

Do you replace washed-out culverts?

Yes. If a culvert under your driveway or lane has collapsed or is too small for the runoff it handles, we dig it out and set a properly sized replacement with the right grade and backfill.

How long does a drainage project take?

Most residential drainage fixes finish in a few days. The variables are access, the amount of rock we hit digging the trench, and how far the water needs to be carried to a safe outlet.
