Ground that runs wet and hills that run steep

Parkersburg sits at the meeting of the Ohio and Little Kanawha Rivers, and that means two things for anyone digging here: a high water table close to the rivers, and hills that climb quickly once you leave the flats. Both shape how we plan a job, so we treat them as part of the site rather than an obstacle to work around.

On low ground we check where water sits before we cut, and we grade so surface water moves away from a slab, garage, or barn instead of pooling against it. On the hills the soil is thinner and rock sits close to the surface, so we pace the work to match what the blade actually finds.

Clearing and building on Wood County land

Most land clearing in Wood County means turning a tree-covered or overgrown piece back into buildable ground. That's cutting and hauling brush, pulling stumps and roots, and leaving the soil shaped so it drains. It also means getting the grade right afterward, because a cleared slope will shed more water than a wooded one did.

For new construction we handle the full sequence — rough grade, cut and fill, and a compacted building pad held to the right height. Outlying acreage in Vienna, Williamstown, Mineral Wells and Davisville gets the same care as an in-town lot; the difference is usually just more ground to grade.

Questions we hear around Parkersburg

Do you really take jobs on the West Virginia side?

Yes, and regularly. Wood, Tyler, and Pleasants County projects are a normal part of our week, not a one-off favor — the crew simply crosses the river the same way our Ohio customers do.

The ground here holds water. Will that sink the project?

It changes how we grade, not whether the job can happen. We check the water table first, then shape the ground and add drainage where needed so water moves away instead of sitting where you don't want it.

Can you get machinery into a yard with only alley access?

Older blocks in Parkersburg often only have a narrow alley to the back, so we fit the machine to the access. Smaller equipment and a planned route in and out keep fences, garages, and neighbor yards intact.

What happens when you hit rock on the hills?

We expect rock on the slopes above the river and plan for it. When we find it we tell you right away and work out the best way through, so surprises don't stall the job or the budget.
