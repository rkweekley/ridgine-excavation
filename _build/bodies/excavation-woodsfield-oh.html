Long gravel lanes into the back acreage

Monroe County property often runs deep from the road, reached by a single gravel lane winding past timber and pasture. Those lanes take every freeze-thaw cycle, and once they rut, getting to the back of your land is a chore. We install and refurbish gravel lanes with a crowned base and ditch drainage, and we'll get a fully loaded dump truck to the back forty without tearing up the ground.

Reclaiming overgrown pasture and old fence lines

Brush and scrub reclaim pasture fast here — give a field a few seasons without grazing or mowing and it's half gone. We clear fence lines, pull multiflora rose and saplings, and push back the tree line so your fields open back up. The ground gets graded behind the clearing, not left as a debris pile.

Ponds and spring-fed wet ground

Spring seeps and wet-weather streams crisscross Monroe County, and land that stays damp into July needs drainage before it's useful. We site and dig recreation ponds on ground that's already holding water, cut drainage around ponds that overflow where they shouldn't, and grade wet fields so water has a path off. We also reopen old oil-and-gas lease roads — the base is still under the brush, and once cleared and graded, you gain access you didn't have.

Excavation questions Woodsfield landowners ask us

My back field is wet all summer from a spring seep — can you drain it?

We locate the seep and the low ground it feeds, then cut drainage — a swale, a French drain or a combination — that gives the water a path off the field. Once graded to drain, it becomes usable pasture or hay ground.

Can you get a machine down a quarter-mile gravel lane to my back acreage?

That's the standard job in Monroe County. We bring the machines down the lane, and if it's rutted or washed, we dress the base as we go so the lane ends up better than when we arrived. We plan access first.

There's an old gas-well road on my property — can you reopen it?

Yes. Most of those lease roads still have a decent base under the brush. We clear the growth, regrade the roadbed, add drainage where needed, and leave you with usable access. Steep sections get crowned and ditched so they don't wash out.

Can you clear and grade a pond site on steep ground near Lewisville?

Yes. Steep ground makes a pond more involved — we build a dam or cut into the slope — but Monroe County's clay subsoil holds water well once shaped. We'll walk the site and explain what the ground needs before we break dirt.
