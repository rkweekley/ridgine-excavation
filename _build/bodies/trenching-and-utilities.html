Utility locates before any dig

Every trenching job starts the same way: we call in 811 utility locates and honor every mark before we put a bucket in the soil. Gas, electric, water, sewer and communications lines run under more of the Mid-Ohio Valley than most people expect, and hitting one is dangerous, expensive, and avoidable.

The marks show where existing lines run so we can plan around them — and where we must hand-dig when a new line crosses an old one. We don't dig blind, and we don't rush the locate window.

Trench depth, width, bedding and backfill

A utility trench isn't just a hole. Water lines go below frost depth so they can't freeze and split during an Ohio winter, and electric conduit sits at its own required depth. The trench has to be wide enough to bed the line properly and deep enough to stay protected once the ground is back over it.

We bed the line in clean material — sand or fine stone, not rocks — so nothing punctures it, then backfill in lifts and tamp each one so the trench doesn't settle into a sunken stripe through your yard.

Running water and power to barns and outbuildings

Rural acreage in the valley often has a barn, shop, or outbuilding that's never been hooked up. We trench the run from the meter, the well or the main line out to the structure, working around the driveway, existing lines and any septic field along the way. Water line installation digging and electrical conduit runs both come down to a clean, straight trench at the right depth, with the line protected.

  • Water line installation digging to a barn, shop, or new build
  • Electrical conduit trenching for power to outbuildings and gates
  • Drainage tile runs to move water off fields and building pads
  • Trenching around existing lines and septic fields, done with care

Working around what's already underground

Established properties carry a tangle of existing lines, and a septic field is ground you don't cut through. We map the locate marks, route the trench clear of the drain field and other buried services, and hand-dig the crossing points where a new line has to pass close to an old one.

Common Questions About Trenching & Utilities

Who calls 811 before you dig?

We do. We call in the utility locates for every trenching job and wait for the marks to come back before we start digging. You don't have to handle the locate process — it's part of how we work.

How deep does a water line need to go?

Below frost depth, so winter ground movement can't reach it. We dig to the depth your line needs to stay safe and freeze-free, and we'll confirm the exact depth for your run.

Can you trench near my septic field?

We work around it. A drain field is ground you don't cut through, so we route the trench clear of it and keep crossings to a minimum.

How do you leave the yard after trenching?

We backfill in compacted lifts and grade the surface level. A fresh trench will settle a little over the first year, and we build it so that settlement stays even instead of becoming a sunken stripe.
