Clay and shale ground that holds water in the fields

Noble County's soil runs to clay and shale, and clay doesn't shed water — it holds it. After a wet stretch, fields stay soft and low spots stay wet for weeks, so drainage work comes first on most jobs here. We grade field drainage, cut swales and set culverts so the water has an actual path off your land. Ponds on this ground are the same story: without drainage around them, they silt in and overflow where they shouldn't.

Long gravel lanes to the back acreage

Most farm property in the county is reached by a single gravel lane winding out past the fields. Those lanes take a beating from frost heave and spring mud, and once they rut, the whole property is harder to work. We install and refurbish gravel lanes with a base that drains, and we clear and reopen old gas-well lease roads that have grown over.

Opening up old fields and overgrown pasture

Brush takes over neglected pasture and field edges fast here. We clear fence lines, pull stumps and push back the brush so your fields are farmable again, and we leave the ground graded rather than a pile of debris you have to handle yourself.

Excavation questions Caldwell landowners ask us

Our farm pond keeps overflowing into the field — can you regrade it?

Usually the pond itself is fine; it's the outflow and drainage around it that are wrong. We cut a proper outlet, set a spillway that handles heavy rain, and grade around the pond so water goes where you want it instead of into the crop ground.

My back field is only reachable by a long, rutted gravel lane. Can you get back there?

That's the everyday situation in Noble County. We bring the machines down the lane, and if it's washed or rutted, we rework the base so it sheds water and stays solid. We plan the access before we move a single load of dirt.

How much does all the shale under our ground affect a dig?

Shale is everywhere out here, so a rocky site means more machine time than plain soil — but it also makes a firm base once it's handled right. We'll tell you what we find as we open the ground, and we'll grade on top of it so your pad stays level.

Can you prep a homesite on the hill above the farm?

Yes. We clear the site, cut a level pad, and put in the drainage and a drive a hilltop build needs. On clay and shale, moving water away from the pad is the whole job, and that's our focus from the first cut.
