Flat ground by the river, steep ground above it

Belpre hugs the Ohio River along Route 7, and the land here changes fast as you move away from the water. Near the river it's low, soft floodplain ground that can hold water; a short climb up the ridge and you're into steeper wooded lots where sandstone and clay sit close to the surface. We've worked both, and each one changes how we approach a project.

On the flats we watch the water table and grade so runoff moves away from where you're building. On the ridge we plan around rock and slope, because cutting into a hillside changes how the whole lot drains once the brush and stumps are gone. The first real step on any Belpre job is understanding which kind of ground we're actually on.

Clearing and site prep along the corridor

Land clearing here tends to be one of two things: reclaiming an overgrown in-town lot, or opening up a wooded slope for a build or a better view. Either way we cut and haul the trees and brush, pull the stumps and roots, and leave ground that's shaped to drain rather than just cleared and left.

After clearing, site prep follows a steady order — rough grade, cut and fill to bring the pad up or down, and a compacted building area that stays true. Tight access on older in-town streets and long gravel pulls aren't a problem; we fit the machine to the lane and plan the route. We serve Vincent, Little Hocking, and Coolville the same way we serve Belpre itself.

Questions we hear around Belpre

Do you work on the West Virginia side too?

Yes. Parkersburg is right across the river, and we cross for Wood County work regularly — see our Parkersburg page.

Is floodplain ground okay to build on?

It's buildable if it's graded and drained right. We check how high the water table sits and shape the pad so water runs off and away, and we'll tell you plainly if raising the grade is the smarter move.

Can you fit equipment down older, narrow streets?

Belpre has plenty of tight streets and short driveways, so we bring machines sized for the access and plan our route in and out to protect curbs, fences, and the neighbor's yard.

What about the sandstone and rock in the hills?

It's common on the ridge, and we plan for it going in. When we hit rock we stop and talk to you about the best way through it, so it doesn't silently grow into a bigger job.
