Why a gravel pad needs more than stone

Mid-Ohio Valley clay doesn't sit still under a load. It swells when wet and shrinks when dry, and a pad built straight on top of it pumps stone into the mud over a few freeze-thaw cycles — a smooth parking area turns into a rutted, sinking patch.

Every gravel pad installation begins by stripping topsoil and organics down to firm mineral ground; that black layer decomposes and settles, and leaving it guarantees low spots. Then we grade the sub-base to shed water, add a compacted layer of larger stone, and cap it with crushed limestone that locks together under compaction.

How a parking area or equipment pad gets built

Site evaluation comes first. We look at where water runs now and where it'll run once the pad is in — a parking area on the low side of a slope needs a cut-off swale uphill. We also check how a stone truck gets in and out without tearing up your lane.

The build layers go in one at a time: a sub-base of larger crushed stone compacted, then a finish surface of smaller stone that packs tight. Drainage is built into the grade, so the pad carries water to its edges instead of pooling.

What makes a pad sink on clay

Water is what does it. Clay absorbs moisture and loses strength, so a pad that held through August ruts by March. Without a sub-base bridging the clay and drainage moving water away, stone sinks into the soil. The fix is building from the bottom up: strip the organics, compact the subgrade, and give water somewhere to go besides under the pad.

Common Questions About Gravel Pads

How thick should a gravel pad be?

It depends on what's under it and what's parking on it. A car or pickup pad on firm ground may need 6 inches of compacted stone; a heavy equipment pad on clay often needs 10 to 14 with a larger stone sub-base.

Why does my gravel parking area have potholes and ruts?

Water sitting under the stone softens the ground, and traffic pushes the stone into it. Without drainage and a proper sub-base, ruts come back every wet season.

Can you build a pad for an RV or camper?

Yes. We build level, compacted pads with drainage so your RV sits on a dry surface that doesn't turn to mud at the edges.

How do you get stone trucks onto a rural lot?

We plan the access route before the first load arrives. On narrow lanes or steep drives we stage deliveries with the driver so the truck doesn't get stuck or tear up ground outside the job.
